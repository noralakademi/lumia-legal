The App can be used without creating an account (anonymously). Even in anonymous use, an anonymous user ID is assigned to your device.

2.0 First, the most important thing: where your data lives

Your health records are not kept on our servers; they stay on your device. The only things on the server are your account (your email address, your nickname, your session and security logs, and a copy of your language preference) and your consent record.

Once you understand this distinction, the rest of this notice is much easier to read:

Where What it covers
Your health records Only in a database on your phone Your symptoms, your cycle, your hormone/medication regimen and dose records, your sleep diary, your reminders, the articles you read, your stage and your stage assessment

What this means in practice: when you log a symptom, mark a dose time, or fill in your sleep diary, that data does not reach us. There is no copy of it on our server; not even an encrypted one, none at all. That is why the tracking side of the App works without an internet connection.

Why we keep the consent record on the server: we have to be able to demonstrate that you gave your explicit consent to the processing of health data; and that you withdrew it, if you do (Article 6/2 of KVKK). That record consists of which consent, which version, and when; it contains none of your health entries.

There is a cost to this and we state it plainly: if you lose or replace your phone, or delete the App, your records go with it; we cannot restore them for you, because we have no copy. That is why the App offers you a way to take your own backup (§6).

6. Backups and exporting your data

Because your tracking records (symptoms, cycle, regimen, sleep…) exist only on your device, the App gives you a way to take your own backup in case you lose your phone. Here is how it works, stated without downplaying or overstating anything:

You create the backup:

  • When you create a backup, you set a password. The backup file is encrypted with that password (AES-256-GCM; the key is derived from your password using Argon2id).
  • Only you know this password. We do not store it and our server never sees it. If you forget it, nobody can open the backup; we cannot either, and we cannot recover it.
  • The file is a real .zip file; its contents are encrypted. Mail programs cannot display such an attachment inline in the message body, and the file's unencrypted outer container carries no explanatory text; the names and counts of your records stay inside the encryption.

You can obtain the backup in two ways:

  1. By sharing the file; you save it from your phone's share sheet to a destination you choose (Files, a cloud service, another device). On this path the file never passes through us.
  2. By sending it to your own email address; our server takes the file and sends it to your address via our email provider (Brevo).

Exactly what happens on the second path:
What is sent Your encrypted backup file, your email address, and the subject line of the email
Who it is sent to Only the verified email address of your own account. The App does not supply the address; the server derives it from your own session credentials. If you have not verified your email address, this path does not work
Can we open the file No; the password is only yours. Neither we nor the provider can read the contents
Do we keep a copy No. The server takes the file, hands it to the provider, and forgets it: it is written to no storage, to no database table, and its contents are recorded in no log
Where it is processed The server function is pinned to the Frankfurt (Germany) region; the email provider is hosted in Belgium; so both are within the European Union

We are not overstating this; the remaining risks:

  • Even encrypted, this is a transfer of personal data. The fact that we cannot open the file does not mean, in legal terms, that we are not processing data.
  • The delivery is not anonymous. The sender address, the subject line and the file name identify Lumia. So even though the email provider cannot see inside the file, it does learn that "this address uses Lumia."
  • The email provider's own delivery logs are not within our control.
  • The file stays in your mailbox. Even if you delete your account, we cannot delete that copy; it is yours now.

7. Cross-border transfer

Our goal is to keep user data in Türkiye; today's infrastructure is abroad, and we say so plainly:

  • Your tracking records (symptoms, cycle, regimen, sleep) never go abroad; because they never go to a server at all (§2.0). This section is not about your health diary.
  • The data processed abroad is your account data (§2.2) and the infrastructure records (§2.4). They are processed in the Frankfurt (Germany) region; the server function that runs when you email yourself a backup is pinned to the same region, and our email provider is in Belgium (§6); both within the European Union.
  • The legal basis is your explicit consent (Article 9 of KVKK). We ask for your consent to the processing of your account data abroad as a separate item on the consent screen, and we keep a record of it. Because our server is abroad, the practical way to withdraw this consent is to delete your account (§8); we are not hiding that.
  • Given the nature of this App, the mere fact that "an email address is registered with Lumia" may itself amount to a statement about health; that is why the transferred set is deliberately kept minimal (email + consent record), and your health diary is never moved abroad under any circumstances.

8. How long do we keep your data?

  • The health records on your device stay on your device until you delete them. Deleting the App also removes them.
  • Your account data is kept until you close your account.
  • Your session and security logs (§2.4; your IP address, sign-in and sign-up events) are kept for at most 90 days, and entries past that period are deleted on a regular schedule. The infrastructure providers' own operational logs and disaster-recovery backups are outside this; they follow the provider's own periods (see the note below).
  • When you delete your account, two things happen together: 1. Your account on the server and all records linked to it are deleted (the one exception is the consent record, two bullets below); 2. The health database on your device is destroyed as well. If the deletion is interrupted (for example if you close the App), the remaining part is completed the next time the App opens. The operation cannot be undone.
  • Your consent records are kept for 5 more years after your account is deleted, and are erased at the end of that period. The basis is Art. 5/2-e: we must be able to prove, even against a claim made after the account is closed, that consent was given and withdrawn. The record holds none of your health data; once the account is deleted it no longer contains your email address; only the random user ID (UUID) and the consent metadata remain.

There are two places deletion does not reach, and we say so plainly:

  1. The infrastructure provider's disaster-recovery backups; copies in those backups remain for their own retention periods.
  2. The backup files you took yourself; an encrypted backup in your mailbox or saved to your own cloud remains in place even if you delete your account. Deleting it is up to you.

9. How is your data protected?

For the data on your device:

  • The health database file sits in the App's own protected area; other apps on your phone cannot read it.
  • Your phone's device encryption covers this file too; so while your phone is locked, the file is encrypted.
  • An honest limit: the database file itself is not additionally encrypted with a separate password on top of device encryption. We plan to add this later.
  • Anyone who knows your phone's passcode can open the App. The App does not currently have its own separate passcode or biometric lock.

For the data on the server:

  • Each user can access only their own data. This is enforced at the database level with Row Level Security; so even a bug in the application code cannot expose another user's data.

We do not promise absolute security for any system; the above are the concrete measures we take.

11. What happens if you withdraw consent?

When you withdraw your explicit consent to the processing of health data, this is what happens; the list below describes what the App actually does:

What is deleted:

  • The personalization and tracking features that rely on this data stop working.
  • All of your health records on the device are deleted: your symptoms, your cycle, your hormone/medication regimen and dose records, your sleep diary, your reminders, the record of which articles you read, and your stage and stage assessment. (We count the reading record too, because §2.1 treats it as special category data; we do not carve out an exception to our own rule.)
  • Your scheduled reminder notifications are cancelled (titles you wrote yourself will not appear on your phone again) and the backup reminder is turned off.

What is not deleted; and why:

  • Your account is not deleted. Your email address, your password and your session stay as they are; this is not an account deletion. If you also want to delete your account, that is a separate route (§8).
  • Your consent record stays on the server, and a "withdrawn" entry is added on top of it (the existing record is not altered). We do this because we have to be able to show that you gave consent and that you withdrew it (Article 6/2 of KVKK; §2.0). That record contains none of your health data.
  • Your language preference and app settings (for example the length of the breathing exercise) are kept; they are not health data and deleting them would gain you nothing.

Withdrawal does not affect processing that was lawful before the date of withdrawal. You can change your mind again: after withdrawing, the App takes you back to the consent screen; if you want, you can consent again from there and start over; but the deleted records do not come back.

Backup files you took earlier remain in your hands; deleting them is up to you.
